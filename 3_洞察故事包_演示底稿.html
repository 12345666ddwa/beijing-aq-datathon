0. 一句话结论(电梯陈述,开场 15 秒)

我们用北京 12 个国控监测站 3 年半的逐小时"当前时刻"观测(污染物 PM10/SO2/NO2/CO/O3 + 气象 TEMP/PRES/DEWP/RAIN/wd/WSPM, 共 11 类原始读数、衍生 119 维特征),训练了一个随机森林模型,预测"下一小时"的 PM2.5 浓度——在严格按时间切分的验证集上误差 RMSE = 32.8 μg/m³(平均绝对误差仅 16.4),比"天天报均值"的朴素基线(102.0)误差降低约 68%,并且完全不需要 PM2.5 自己的历史数据。 这套方法回答了主办方提出的三个问题: ①什么在驱动 PM2.5 → 现时污染物(PM10 与 CO),②污染模式如何随站点与季节变化 → 城区比郊区高约 28%,供暖季夜间是超标主窗口,③对监测与公共健康规划的启示 → PM10 可当预警代理、供暖季傍晚提前 1 小时滚动预警。

1. 发现一:什么在驱动 PM2.5?——"现时污染物"是压倒性的预报因子

1.1 故事线(给评委讲人话)

PM2.5 不是凭空出现的。北京冬天的霾,主要来自燃烧过程:电厂与供暖锅炉、机动车尾气、工业与扬尘。这些过程同时排出 PM10(粗颗粒)、CO(一氧化碳)、NO2(二氧化氮)等——它们与 PM2.5 "同源共变"。因此,你不需要等到 PM2.5 仪器读数出来,只要看到同一时刻的 PM10、CO 飙升,就能推断下一小时 PM2.5 大概率也要升。这,就是整个预测模型的物理基础。

1.2 双重证据:数据相关 + 模型重要性,两条独立路线指向同一答案

证据 A — 相关性(EDA, Pearson 相关, 逐对剔除缺失):

一句话总结给评委: 下一小时的 PM2.5, 主要由"现在的 PM10 和 CO"决定; 风与湿度等气象条件是第二位的调节因子。

2. 发现二:空间的差异——城区与郊区是两个世界, 一个"背景站"代表不了全城

2.1 数字事实(12 站全期均值, μg/m³)

组 站点(均值) 特征
城区核心 Dongsi 83.7(最脏) / Nongzhanguan 82.7 / Wanshouxigong 82.4 / Wanliu 82.2 / Gucheng 81.6 / Aotizhongxin 81.1 / Guanyuan 80.9 / Tiantan 80.1 8 个城区站高度抱团: 80.1–83.7, 组内极差仅 3.6
过渡/近郊 Shunyi 77.9 介于两者之间
郊区背景 Changping 69.8 / Huairou 68.9 / Dingling 65.2(最干净) 65–70
  • 最脏的城区站(Dongsi 83.7)比最干净的郊区背景站(Dingling 65.2)高 18.5 μg/m³, 约 28%(EDA 原话: 极差≈18.5 μg/m³, 约 28%)。
  • 12 站分布形状相似(均右偏, std 70–83), 差别主要在均值水平。
  • 有趣的是: 站点编号在模型特征重要性中仅排第 45 位(0.13%)——站与站的差别, 本质上已被"该站此刻的污染物读数"吸收了: 城区站之所以脏, 是因为它的 PM10/CO 本来就高。也就是说, 有了实时污染物, "你在哪个站"几乎不增加预测信息。

2.2 对监测网络布局的启示(评委加分点)

  1. 郊区背景站不能代表城区人口暴露: 若拿 Dingling(65.2)代表全北京, 而城区 8 站均值约 82(81.8, 本次算得)、最脏站 83.7——城区真实暴露会被系统性低估约 18.5 μg/m³, 相当于城区水平的 23%, 足以影响污染等级判定与健康预警。健康预警必须用城区站(或城区站为主)的数据。
  2. 城区站之间有冗余但仍是必需的: 8 个城区站高度一致(极差 3.6), 说明城区污染是区域性的, 单站故障时邻居站可做交叉校验/替补; 郊区背景站则承担"区域传输与本底评估"的独特角色——两类站职责不同, 都应保留。
  3. 站点浓度差 ≈18.5 μg/m³ 且是系统性、常年存在的(不是偶发)——暴露评估与流行病学研究若混用站点, 会引入约两成的系统偏差, 研究设计必须按城区/郊区分层。

支撑证据: EDA_report.md §2 站点表与解读; rf_raw_importance.csv(station_code 0.13%); 图 fig2_station_means.png。

3. 发现三:时间维度——"供暖季 + 夜晚"是超标的主窗口

3.1 季节:冬天是夏天的约 1.8 倍

全期 12 站合并的自然月均值(μg/m³):

1月 2月 3月 4月 5月 6月 7月 8月 9月 10月 11月 12月
87.1 93.5 94.7 72.7 63.0 69.1 71.8 53.4(最低) 63.9 94.3 92.0 96.0(最高)
  • 采暖季(10–3 月)系统性高于暖季(5–8 月); 12 月均值 96.0 是 8 月 53.4 的 1.8 倍。
  • 但要警惕"年际陷阱": 最极端的两个月份是 2015-12(150.1)与 2016-02(42.9)——同一个"2 月", 2014 年 148.3、2016 年只有 42.9。纯靠"几月"做预测会被年际漂移击穿(基线 B2"站点×月份均值"RMSE 107.8, 比常数基线 102.0 还差!)——所以模型必须主要靠实时观测特征, 日历只做先验。(EDA §3、§7 B2)

3.2 日变化:晚间是"爬坡期", 供暖季尤其陡(本次从 train.csv 现算)

注: EDA 报告未含日内分析, 以下数字为本次直接对 train.csv(360,954 行)按小时分组现算的描述统计(按观测时刻 h 计, 目标为该时刻之后 1 小时的 PM2.5; 全期 12 站合并)。

时段 全体年份 采暖月(11/12/1/2月) 暖月(5–8月)
全天最低 15 时 72.4 13 时 78.3 17 时 60.3
全天最高 20 时 86.2 21 时 112.5 8 时 68.8
晚间 20–23 时均值 ~85–86 ~110–113 ~64
峰/谷比 1.19 1.44 1.14
  • 模式一(全体年份): 下午 15–16 时是一天最低点(大气扩散最好的时段), 之后持续爬升, 20–21 时达到峰值——排放一天累积 + 夜间边界层压低、扩散转差的典型表现。
  • 模式二(供暖季) : 午后 13 时谷底 78.3 之后一路爬升, 21 时冲到 112.5(比谷底高 44%), 20–23 时连续 4 个小时维持在 110–113——供暖燃烧叠加夜间静稳, 是全天最脏的窗口。
  • 模式三(暖季) : 全天平缓(60–69), 仅清晨 6–8 时缓慢抬升出现一个小峰(68.8), 下午 15–17 时最低——与夏季边界层高、对流强、无供暖的物理直觉一致。
  • ⚠️ 需要说明: 模型的特征重要性显示小时级日历特征本身权重很低(weekday 0.15%, dayofyear 类 0.7–0.8%)——日变化信号由实时污染物+气象特征承载(它们在晚间本来就高), 模型学到的是"成因"而非"钟点"。这正是我们想展示的: 模型在模仿物理过程, 而不是死记日历。

4. 公共健康与管理启示(5 条可落地建议)

① 用 PM10 做 PM2.5 的低成本"代理预警指标"。 PM10 与下一小时 PM2.5 相关 r=+0.85, 且是模型第一特征(72.2% 重要性);而 PM10 数据缺失率仅 0.5%, 远低于 CO(4.4%)。当 PM2.5 监测仪器维护/故障, 或预算有限只能装单通道传感器时,用 PM10 读数(必要时叠加 CO)即可可靠推断 PM2.5 走势——城市可借此降低监测网络成本、提高容灾能力。 (证据: EDA §4 相关表、§5 缺失表; rf_raw_importance.csv)

② 供暖季"傍晚—夜间"是提前预警的最佳窗口。 采暖月 21 时平均 112.5 μg/m³(约为全体数据日均浓度 78 的 1.4 倍), 20–23 时连续处于 110+;12 月均值(96)是 8 月(53)的 1.8 倍。建议: 供暖启动(11 月)前后发布季节性健康提示;每日 17–20 时基于实时 PM10/CO 做 1 小时滚动预警, 敏感人群(老人、儿童、哮喘患者)错峰户外活动——本模型输入全部是"当前时刻可观测值", 天然支持逐小时滚动发布。 (证据: 日变化本次现算表; EDA §3 月度表)

③ 以城区站为准发布暴露预警, 郊区背景站用于区域评估。 城区 8 站抱团在 80.1–83.7, 郊区背景站低至 65.2, 与最脏城区站相差 18.5 μg/m³(相对城区均值约 23%)。用郊区站代表全市会系统性低估人口暴露;预警应以城区站为主, 郊区背景站继续承担区域传输与本底监测职能;城区站间高度一致也意味着单站故障可用邻近站插补。 (证据: EDA §2 站点表; 本节 2.2)

④ 监测网络的运维优先级: CO 通道最不能坏。 CO 是模型第二重要特征(10.1%), 却是全数据缺失率最高的污染物列(4.4%, 其次 O3 2.4%、NO2 2.1%)。现实部署中CO 通道故障会直接削弱 1 小时预警的精度;数据管线应对 CO 缺失做实时插补(可用 PM10/NO2 关联), 仪器巡检优先保障 CO。 (证据: EDA §5 缺失表; rf_raw_importance.csv)

⑤ "无 PM2.5 历史也能预测"= 可独立交叉验证的预警体系。 由于数据设计上就不含 current_PM2_5(防泄漏红线), 本模型的预测完全建立在其他污染物与气象之上——这意味着: 即使某站 PM2.5 传感器全线故障, 只要 PM10/CO/气象还在工作, 预警照常运行; 反过来, 用模型预测与真实 PM2.5 对照, 还能反向校验 PM2.5 仪器漂移。一套数据, 两个用途。 (证据: EDA §0(无 current_PM2_5 列); log_rf_raw.txt 精度; 方法见 §5)

5. 方法与严谨性说明(让评委信任结果)

  1. 严格时间切分, 杜绝"偷看未来": 按时间先后把数据切成 训练 2013-03→2015-08(257,927 行)/ 验证 2015-09→2016-02(51,349 行),2016-03 之后的 51,678 行整段丢弃;验证期特意选与测试集同季节的供暖季。随机打乱做 K 折在这里是错的——相邻小时高度相关, 会系统性高估精度, 因此弃用。(EDA §7; log_rf_raw.txt)
  2. 特征只用"当下与过去": 全部 119 维特征 = 当前时刻污染物/气象 + 它们的历史滞后/滚动窗口(lag1–24h、roll3–24h)+ 日历, 没有任何未来信息;缺失值用仅在训练段估计的中位数填充(不泄漏验证期)。滞后特征按"小时网格对齐"构造(数据有约 2,466 个缺测小时, 若按行位移会使缺测后所有滞后错位——已重写为按时间戳对齐并 21 点抽查通过)。(STATUS.md P1-A; train_rf_spec.md)
  3. 模型可解释、结论可交叉验证: Random Forest 输出每个变量的贡献排序, 与 EDA 的独立相关分析互相印证(PM10: 相关 0.85 ↔ 重要性 72%; CO: 0.76 ↔ 10%; 风速: 负相关 ↔ 正向重要性)。两条独立证据链指向同一物理故事。
  4. 目标变换实验做了, 输了, 如实报告: 目标是强右偏分布(均值 78 > 中位数 55, 偏度 2.01, 峰值 999 封顶仅 1 行), 我们测定了 Box-Cox 变换参数(全样本 MLE λ=0.167; 与队友 R 的 Guerrero λ≈0.09 同量级)并跑了完整对照实验——结果 raw 直接回归 RMSE 32.8 反而优于 Box-Cox 的 38.1。结论: 对树模型+原始尺度 RMSE 评分, 单调变换的收益抵不过逆变换误差。这个"负面结果"反而说明我们用证据而不是习惯做选择。
  5. 基线锚定, 精度可解读: 验证集标准差 102.0 → "预测均值"的理论 RMSE 下限就是 102(常数基线实测 102.04, 与主办方占位常数 78.04 逻辑一致);线性模型(无任何滞后)已到 36.1;我们的 RF 32.8 是在"线性已逼近信号上限"的背景下再挤出 9.1% 的提升, 且 MAE 仅 16.4(约为平均浓度 78 的 21%)。对一个动态范围到 999、75% 分位才 108 的重尾系统, 这个误差水平意味着平均而言预测偏差小于一个空气质量指数档位。
  6. 诚实披露局限: ①目标重尾(均值 78 vs 中位数 55, >600 的 197 行占 0.055%), RMSE 评分下极端霾事件的平方误差权重极高, 是模型最大的挑战; ②验证覆盖 2015-09 → 2016-02(供暖季主体, 与测试同季), 春夏外推能力由测试集(2016-09→2017-02)检验; ③特征重要性是置换型近似, 对高相关特征组(PM10/CO 同源)会偏向其一——这正是我们同时展示相关表的原因。

0. The One-Sentence Takeaway (Elevator Statement, Opening 15 Seconds)

Using 3.5 years of hourly "current-moment" observations from Beijing's 12 national air-quality monitoring stations (pollutants PM10/SO2/NO2/CO/O3 + meteorology TEMP/PRES/DEWP/RAIN/wd/WSPM — 11 types of raw readings, from which 119 features were derived), we trained a random forest model to forecast the PM2.5 concentration of the "next hour". On a strictly time-split validation set the error is RMSE = 32.8 μg/m³ (mean absolute error of just 16.4) — an ~68% reduction in error versus the naive "always predict the mean" baseline (102.0) — and it needs no historical PM2.5 data of its own. This approach answers the three questions posed by the organizers: ① What drives PM2.5 → co-emitted pollutants (PM10 and CO). ② How pollution patterns vary across stations and seasons → urban is ~28% higher than suburban, and heating-season nights are the primary window for standard exceedances. ③ Implications for monitoring and public-health planning → PM10 can serve as an early-warning proxy, with rolling warnings issued 1 hour ahead on heating-season evenings.

1. Finding One: What Drives PM2.5? — "Co-emitted Pollutants" Are the Overwhelmingly Dominant Predictors

1.1 The Storyline (Plain Language for the Judges)

PM2.5 does not appear out of thin air. Beijing's winter haze comes mainly from combustion processes: power plants and heating boilers, vehicle exhaust, industry, and fugitive dust. These processes simultaneously emit PM10 (coarse particles), CO (carbon monoxide), NO2 (nitrogen dioxide), and more — they "co-vary with PM2.5 because they share the same sources". Therefore, you do not need to wait for the PM2.5 instrument reading: the moment you see PM10 and CO spike in the same hour, you can infer that next-hour PM2.5 is likely to rise as well. That is the physical foundation of the entire forecasting model.

2.2 Implications for Monitoring-Network Design (Bonus Points with the Judges)

  1. Suburban background stations cannot represent urban population exposure: if Dingling (65.2) were taken to represent all of Beijing, while the 8 urban stations average about 82 (81.8, computed for this report) and the dirtiest station is 83.7 — true urban exposure would be systematically underestimated by ~18.5 μg/m³, equivalent to 23% of urban levels, enough to affect pollution-grade classification and health warnings. Health warnings must use urban station data (or be primarily based on it).
  2. Urban stations are redundant with one another yet still necessary: the 8 urban stations are highly consistent (range of 3.6), showing that urban pollution is regional — if one station fails, neighboring stations can cross-check or substitute; the suburban background stations play the unique role of "regional transport and background assessment" — the two station types have different jobs; both should be kept.
  3. The station concentration gap of ≈18.5 μg/m³ is systematic and perennial (not occasional) — exposure assessments and epidemiological studies that mix stations would introduce roughly a 20% systematic bias; research designs must stratify by urban/suburban.

Supporting evidence: station table and interpretation in EDA_report.md §2; rf_raw_importance.csv (station_code 0.13%); figure fig2_station_means.png.

3. Finding Three: The Temporal Dimension — "Heating Season + Night" Is the Main Window for High Concentrations

3.1 Seasonality: Winter Is ~1.8× Summer

Full-period, 12-station pooled calendar-month means (μg/m³):

Jan Feb Mar Apr May Jun Jul Aug Sep Oct Nov Dec
87.1 93.5 94.7 72.7 63.0 69.1 71.8 53.4 (lowest) 63.9 94.3 92.0 96.0 (highest)
  • The heating season (Oct–Mar) is systematically higher than the warm season (May–Aug); the December mean of 96.0 is 1.8× August's 53.4.
  • But beware the "inter-annual trap": the two most extreme months are 2015-12 (150.1) and 2016-02 (42.9) — in the very same "February", 2014 hit 148.3 while 2016 was only 42.9. Forecasting from "which month" alone would be broken by inter-annual drift (baseline B2, "station × month means", has an RMSE of 107.8 — even worse than the constant baseline of 102.0!). The model must therefore rely primarily on real-time observational features, with the calendar serving only as a prior. (EDA §3, §7 B2)

  • Pattern 2 (heating season): after the 13:00 trough of 78.3, concentrations climb all the way and surge to 112.5 at 21:00 (44% above the trough), holding at 110–113 for four consecutive hours from 20:00 to 23:00 — heating combustion on top of nocturnal stagnation makes this the dirtiest window of the day.

That is exactly what we want to demonstrate: the model mimics the physical process rather than memorizing the calendar.

4. Public-Health and Management Implications (5 Actionable Recommendations)

① Use PM10 as a low-cost "proxy early-warning indicator" for PM2.5. PM10 correlates with next-hour PM2.5 at r=+0.85 and is the model's number-one feature (72.2% importance); PM10 also has a missing-data rate of only 0.5%, far below CO's 4.4%. When PM2.5 monitoring instruments are under maintenance or faulty — or when the budget only allows single-channel sensors — PM10 readings (plus CO when necessary) can reliably infer PM2.5 trends, letting cities cut monitoring-network costs and improve resilience. (Evidence: EDA §4 correlation table, §5 missingness table; rf_raw_importance.csv)

② The heating-season "evening–night" period is the best window for advance warning. Heating months average 112.5 μg/m³ at 21:00 (≈1.4× the all-data daily mean concentration of 78), and 20:00–23:00 stays continuously above 110; the December mean (96) is 1.8× August's (53). Recommendation: issue seasonal health notices around heating start-up (November); run 1-hour rolling warnings between 17:00 and 20:00 each day based on real-time PM10/CO so that sensitive groups (the elderly, children, asthma sufferers) can shift their outdoor activity away from peak hours — because every input to this model is "observable at the current moment", it naturally supports hour-by-hour rolling issuance. (Evidence: diurnal table computed on the fly for this report; EDA §3 monthly table)

③ Issue exposure warnings from the urban stations; keep the suburban background stations for regional assessment. The 8 urban stations cluster at 80.1–83.7, while the suburban background stations go as low as 65.2 — 18.5 μg/m³ below the dirtiest urban station (≈23% relative to the urban mean). Representing the whole city with suburban stations would systematically understate population exposure; warnings should be based primarily on the urban stations, while the suburban background stations continue to serve regional-transport and background monitoring; the high consistency among urban stations also means a single failed station can be filled in by its neighbors. (Evidence: EDA §2 station table; §2.2 of this report)

④ Monitoring-network maintenance priority: the CO channel is the one that must not fail. CO is the model's second-most-important feature (10.1%), yet it is the pollutant column with the highest missing-data rate in the whole dataset (4.4%; next are O3 at 2.4% and NO2 at 2.1%). In real deployments, a CO channel failure would directly weaken the accuracy of 1-hour warnings; the data pipeline should impute missing CO in real time (feasible via its association with PM10/NO2), and instrument inspections should prioritize CO. (Evidence: EDA §5 missingness table; rf_raw_importance.csv)

⑤ "Predicting without PM2.5 history" = an early-warning system that can be independently cross-validated. Because the data design deliberately contains no current_PM2_5 (the anti-leakage red line), this model's forecasts rest entirely on other pollutants and meteorology — which means: even if every PM2.5 sensor at a station fails, as long as PM10/CO/meteorology are still working, warnings keep running as usual. Conversely, comparing model predictions against true PM2.5 provides a reverse check on PM2.5 instrument drift. One dataset, two uses. (Evidence: EDA §0 (no current_PM2_5 column); log_rf_raw.txt accuracy; method described in §5)

5. Methods and Rigor (So That Judges Can Trust the Results)

  1. Strict temporal split — no "peeking into the future": the data are split chronologically into train 2013-03→2015-08 (257,927 rows) / validation 2015-09→2016-02 (51,349 rows); the 51,678 rows after 2016-03 are discarded entirely; the validation window deliberately covers the heating season, the same season as the test set. Randomly shuffled K-fold would be wrong here — adjacent hours are highly correlated and would systematically inflate accuracy, so it was rejected. (EDA §7; log_rf_raw.txt)
  2. Features use only "the present and the past": all 119 features = current-hour pollutant/meteorological readings + their historical lag/rolling windows (lag1–24h, roll3–24h) + calendar — no future information whatsoever; missing values are filled with medians estimated on the training segment only (no leakage into validation). Lag features are constructed on an hour-grid alignment (the data contain ~2,466 missing hours; row-shift construction would misalign every lag after a missing hour — rewritten to align by timestamp and verified by spot checks at 21 time points). (STATUS.md P1-A; train_rf_spec.md)
  3. The model is interpretable and the conclusions can be cross-validated: the random forest outputs a contribution ranking for every variable that corroborates the EDA's independent correlation analysis (PM10: correlation 0.85 ↔ importance 72%; CO: 0.76 ↔ 10%; wind speed: negative correlation ↔ positive importance). Two independent evidence chains point to the same physical story.
  4. The target transformation was tested, it lost, and we report it honestly: the target is strongly right-skewed (mean 78 > median 55, skewness 2.01, max 999 capped for a single row). We estimated the Box-Cox transformation parameters (full-sample MLE λ=0.167; the same order of magnitude as teammate R's Guerrero λ≈0.09) and ran the full controlled comparison — as it turned out, raw direct regression (RMSE 32.8) beats Box-Cox (38.1). Conclusion: for tree models scored on raw-scale RMSE, the gain from a monotone transformation does not offset the inverse-transformation error. This "negative result" actually shows that we choose with evidence rather than by habit.
  5. Baselines anchor the numbers; the accuracy is interpretable: the validation-set std is 102.0 → the theoretical RMSE floor for "always predicting the mean" is 102 (the constant baseline measures 102.04, logically consistent with the organizers' placeholder constant of 78.04); a linear model (without any lags) already reaches 36.1; our RF's 32.8 squeezes out a further 9.1% improvement in a setting where the linear model is already near the signal ceiling, and the MAE is only 16.4 (≈21% of the mean concentration of 78). For a heavy-tailed system whose dynamic range reaches 999 while its 75th percentile is only 108, this error level means the average prediction deviates by less than one air-quality-index band.
  6. Honest disclosure of limitations: ① heavy-tailed target (mean 78 vs median 55; 197 rows above 600, i.e. 0.055%), where under RMSE scoring the squared errors of extreme haze events carry enormous weight — the model's biggest challenge; ② validation covers 2015-09→2016-02 (the bulk of the heating season, same season as the test set), and spring/summer extrapolation is examined by the test set (2016-09→2017-02); ③ feature importance is a permutation-style approximation that will favor one member of highly correlated feature groups (PM10/CO share sources) — which is exactly why we also show the correlation table.

Supporting evidence: EDA_report.md §1/§6/§7; models/log_rf_raw.txt, log_rf_boxcox.txt; STATUS.md (record of the lag grid-alignment fix); reports/train_rf_spec.md.
